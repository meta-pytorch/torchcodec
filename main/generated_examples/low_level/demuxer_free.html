Decoding packets that don’t come from a Demuxer#

Important

The low-level APIs are in beta. Their signatures and semantics may still change slightly, in response to user feedback. Please share your feedback!

Sometimes, you don’t have a file to demux. Maybe your packets were demuxed ahead of time and stored in a database, maybe they arrive over the network, or maybe they come from a container format that TorchCodec can’t read. You can still decode them: a VideoPacketDecoder doesn’t need a Demuxer. All it needs are the VideoCodecParameters of the stream, and Packet objects, which you can build from the compressed data yourself.

In this tutorial, we’ll decode packets that an earlier job demuxed and stored, without the original file.

Some boilerplate first: a test video, and the device we’ll run on.

import subprocess
import tempfile
from pathlib import Path

import torch


device = "cuda" if torch.cuda.is_available() else "cpu"
print(f"{device = }")

temp_dir = Path(tempfile.mkdtemp())
video_path = temp_dir / "video.mp4"
subprocess.run(
    [
        "ffmpeg", "-y", "-hide_banner", "-loglevel", "error",
        "-f", "lavfi", "-i", "testsrc2=size=640x360:rate=30:duration=3",
        "-c:v", "libx264", "-pix_fmt", "yuv420p", "-g", "30", "-bf", "2",
        str(video_path),
    ],
    check=True,
)

device = 'cuda'

CompletedProcess(args=['ffmpeg', '-y', '-hide_banner', '-loglevel', 'error', '-f', 'lavfi', '-i', 'testsrc2=size=640x360:rate=30:duration=3', '-c:v', 'libx264', '-pix_fmt', 'yuv420p', '-g', '30', '-bf', '2', '/tmp/tmpdae50gr5/video.mp4'], returncode=0)

Storing the packets#

Let’s play the earlier job: it demuxes the video and stores, for each packet, its data, its pts and its flags, which is the data we’ll later need to reconstruct such Packets. It also stores the values a decoder needs about the stream, which we’ll look at below.

from torchcodec.decoders import Demuxer

demuxer = Demuxer(video_path)
stream = demuxer.streams[0]
rows = [(bytes(packet.data.numpy()), packet.pts, packet.flags) for packet in demuxer]
stream_info = {
    "codec": stream.codec_parameters.codec,
    "time_base": stream.codec_parameters.time_base,
    "extradata": stream.codec_parameters.extradata,
    "width": stream.codec_parameters.width,
    "height": stream.codec_parameters.height,
    "pixel_format": stream.codec_parameters.pixel_format,
    "r_frame_rate": stream.codec_parameters.r_frame_rate,
}
print(f"{len(rows)} packets stored")

90 packets stored

From here on, there’s no Demuxer and no file: only rows and stream_info, which play the role of our database.

del demuxer, stream

Codec parameters#

A decoder needs to know about the stream it decodes: its codec, the unit of the packets’ timestamps, and some codec-specific values. That’s what VideoCodecParameters holds. Here, we build them from the stored values:

from torchcodec.decoders import VideoCodecParameters

codec_parameters = VideoCodecParameters(
    stream_info["codec"],
    time_base=stream_info["time_base"],
    extradata=stream_info["extradata"],
    width=stream_info["width"],
    height=stream_info["height"],
    pixel_format=stream_info["pixel_format"],
    r_frame_rate=stream_info["r_frame_rate"],
)
print(codec_parameters)

VideoCodecParameters(codec='h264', time_base=Fraction(1, 15360), extradata=<47 bytes>, width=640, height=360, pixel_format='yuv420p', r_frame_rate=Fraction(30, 1))

Which fields you need depends on the codec. For H.264 in MP4, the decoder needs the extradata: it’s where MP4 stores the codec’s setup data, outside of the packets. To decode on CUDA, width, height and pixel_format are needed too, and r_frame_rate gives the frames their durations. To find out what to set for your own packets, print the codec parameters a Demuxer reports for a similar file: print(Demuxer(path).streams[0].codec_parameters).

Note

If the packets are demuxed with TorchCodec in the first place, as above, it’s simpler and safer to store the whole VideoCodecParameters object, e.g. pickled, rather than a few of its fields: it holds everything the decoder may need, including fields you wouldn’t think of.

Decoding the packets#

Packet.from_bytes() builds a packet from its data, and Packet.from_tensor() does the same from a 1D uint8 tensor. Only the data is required, but the pts is what gives the decoded frames their timestamps. Unlike in most TorchCodec APIs, timestamps are integers, in units of the stream’s time base. The rest is the usual decoding loop:

from torchcodec.decoders import ColorConverter, Packet, VideoPacketDecoder

packets = [Packet.from_bytes(data, pts=pts, flags=flags) for data, pts, flags in rows]

packet_decoder = VideoPacketDecoder(codec_parameters, device=device)
color_converter = ColorConverter(device=device)

frames = []
for packet in packets:
    frames += [color_converter.convert(f) for f in packet_decoder.decode(packet)]
frames += [color_converter.convert(f) for f in packet_decoder.drain()]
print(f"{len(frames)} frames, {frames[0].data.shape = }, {frames[0].data.device = }")

90 frames, frames[0].data.shape = torch.Size([3, 360, 640]), frames[0].data.device = device(type='cuda', index=0)

Matching frames to packets#

The frames don’t come out of the decoder in the order the packets went in. But RawFrame.pts is the pts of the packet the frame was decoded from, and both are integers: a frame can be matched exactly to its packet, e.g. to look up what you stored along with it.

The codec parameters and the packets can also be pickled, e.g. to demux in one process and decode in another: see the last section of Multi-threaded decoding pipelines.
